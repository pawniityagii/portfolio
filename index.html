02

Machine learning · solo

Fraud Detection System

Supervised models that flag fraudulent transactions, built around the fact that fraud data is badly imbalanced and accuracy is the wrong thing to optimise.

Source & notebook

why the metric choice is the whole problem

Cleaned and preprocessed the transaction data, handling missing values, outliers and multicollinearity, then engineered the features that actually carry fraud signal — balance deltas, mismatch errors, ratios, merchant indicators. Compared logistic regression against gradient boosting and LightGBM, and evaluated on ROC-AUC, PR-AUC and precision/recall rather than raw accuracy, because a model that predicts "not fraud" every time scores well on accuracy and is worthless. Finished with an explainability pass so the flags could be justified, not just produced.

03

NLP · unsupervised · solo

Mental Health Sentiment Analysis

TF-IDF and KMeans clustering over mental-health-related text, with a dashboard on top — and a result I ended up arguing with rather than reporting.

Source & notebook

What I found: the clusters separated, but the emotion names I mapped onto them did not survive inspection — "I'm really excited about the weekend" sat in the same cluster as genuinely distressed text. A cluster id is not a sentiment. Catching that in my own output, instead of shipping a clean-looking chart, is the part of this project I would defend in an interview.

Dataset from Kaggle, text cleaning and TF-IDF vectorisation in Python, KMeans for the clustering, PCA to make the separation visible, and Matplotlib and Seaborn for the dashboard views. The honest conclusion is that unsupervised clustering found structure in the text, but not the structure I had assumed, and hand-labelling clusters is exactly where that method breaks.

05

Business intelligence · solo · built for a non-technical audience

Digital Marketing Dashboard

A Power BI dashboard for comparing campaigns and deciding where the next rupee of budget goes — built for someone who needs a decision, not a model.

the argument the dashboard was built to make

I cleaned and processed the raw campaign data, then built CTR, conversion and ROI measures into interactive charts with slicers and filters so campaigns could be compared side by side. The useful part was insisting the metrics be read together: high CTR with low conversions is a post-click problem, not a creative win, and the campaign with a lower CTR but a higher ROI is often the one worth more money. That is an argument you have to make out loud, to someone who controls the budget, without leaning on the word "metric".
